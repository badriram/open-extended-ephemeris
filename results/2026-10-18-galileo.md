# Galileo, from broadcast orbits

`--systems G,E` adds Galileo. There is no anonymous multi-GNSS ultra-rapid orbit, so Galileo's 48 h
arcs are built from its broadcast I/NAV ephemerides (as `--from-broadcast` does for GPS), with the
same force model, fit and 5-day quadratic clock. Output: Galileo records in `gps-7d.rnx` and as
UBX-MGA-GAL-EPH in `gps-7d-ubx.bin` (u-blox); CASIC output stays GPS only: the base AT6558 does not
track Galileo, and CASIC has no documented Galileo ephemeris message for variants that do.

Checked: all 3,019 Galileo I/NAV records in a day of broadcasts land exactly on MGA-GAL-EPH's
grid (Galileo's own scales: toe/toc 60 s, af0 2^-34, af1 2^-46, af2 2^-59, BGD 2^-32); records are
evaluated with Galileo's GM (3.986004418e14, not GPS's 3.986005e14, about 2 m apart after 2 h).

**A bug found on the way**: the Earth-orientation table was sized from the IGS arc (24 h before the
prediction) while broadcast-built arcs start 48 h before, so the first 21 h of every Galileo arc
used a clamped, wrong Earth orientation (fits of ~27 m RMS). The table now covers every track.

**Clocks are excellent** (hydrogen masers and rubidium standards): 1.1 m median clock error at
4.5-5 days against 1.9 m for GPS. **Orbits are the limit**: at day 4-5 along-track 42 m and
cross-track 15 m (median), radial 1.9 m, against 19 m 3D for GPS from broadcasts. Looser, freer or
ECOM1 radiation-pressure priors made no difference, and a 72 h arc was worse. The fits agree
across satellites (D0 about -107 nm/s², B0 about -10 nm/s² on the FOC satellites, smaller on IOV),
which points at Galileo's radiation pressure needing an a priori box-wing model, as the
literature finds for Galileo (Nowak et al. 2023; IGS workshop 2018: 7-day errors roughly halved).
The worst satellites are the IOV ones (E11, E12), a different design.

Not yet in the published files: opt in with `--systems G,E`.

## Box-wing radiation pressure and the antenna offset: physically right, prediction-neutral

**Box-wing a priori model** (`--box-wing`, `src/boxwing.rs`): surfaces, areas and optical
properties (absorption, specular, diffuse) for IOV and FOC from the European GNSS Service Centre's
Galileo satellite metadata (sections 3, 4 and 6), nominal yaw steering, re-emission from the
multi-layer insulation. It accounts for about 89% of the push along the Sun direction (the fitted
ECOM D0 falls from about -107 to about -12 nm/s²), which validates it. But predictions did not
move (backtest 1, Galileo, 4.5-5 days: 9.33 / 23.35 m without, 9.23 / 23.42 m with): the empirical
ECOM terms had already absorbed what it explains.

**Fitted radial antenna offset** (on for broadcast-built tracks; `--no-antenna-offset` to turn it
off): broadcast orbits describe the antenna, about a metre Earthward of the centre of mass, and an
orbit cannot follow a radially shifted point at the true angular rate. Estimating a constant
radial offset in the fit lowered fit residuals (Galileo 0.71 to 0.59 m, GPS from broadcasts 0.87 to
0.38 m) and nudged GPS-from-broadcast predictions (4.28 / 14.1 m to 4.15 / 13.9 m at 4.5-5 days),
but left Galileo's unchanged (9.49 / 23.39 m).

**Where Galileo's error comes from.** Built from broadcasts, GPS and Galileo show the same
pattern, Galileo's about 2.8 times larger at day 4-5:

| | radial | along-track | cross-track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GPS from broadcasts, day 4-5 | 0.61 m | 15.4 m | 5.5 m |
| Galileo, day 4-5 | 1.86 m | 42.8 m | 15.0 m |

So it is mostly the broadcast-orbit input, not a Galileo-specific bug in the force model. A precise
Galileo orbit to start from (as GPS has in the IGS ultra-rapid orbit) is the likely fix; no
anonymous source has been found yet.
