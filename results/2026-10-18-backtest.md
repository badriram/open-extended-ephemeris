# Predicted GPS orbit data: backtest, 2026-10-18

**What was predicted**: the predictor run on the IGS ultra-rapid orbit
`IGS0OPSULT_20262850600_02D_15M_ORB.SP3` (measured 2026-10-12 06:00 to 10-13 06:00 UTC, IGS
prediction to 10-14 06:00) with its ERP file, calibrated against BKG's merged broadcasts of
2026-10-12 and 10-13. Output: 2,720 MSG-GPSEPH frames, 32 satellites, one every 2 h.

(Measured with the first, Python implementation; the Rust program that replaced it the same day
reproduces this table to within 0.03 m, e.g. 4.73 / 17.19 / 25.2 m at 4.5-5 days, in 2 seconds
instead of 5.6 minutes.)

**What it was graded against**: what the satellites actually broadcast from 2026-10-13 to 10-17
(BKG `BRDC00WRD_S`, days 286-290), at 15-minute steps through each predicted record's central
two hours, using the broadcast record a receiver would have used at that moment. The frames were
decoded from the output file, so encoding is included.

**Metric**: signal-in-space range error, `sqrt((0.98 R - c dT)^2 + (A^2 + C^2) / 49)` with R, A, C
the radial, along-track and cross-track orbit differences and dT the clock difference: the part of
a range measurement's error that comes from the satellite's data. Position error is roughly this
times the geometry factor (about 2). The broadcasts themselves are good to about 0.5 m, so values
below that measure agreement with the broadcast rather than truth.

(The URA index column is what that run sent; the table it comes from has since been tightened to
these figures.)

The first version of the tool, graded the same way the same day, reached 10.6 / 53.5 / 66 m at
4.5-5 days with orbits off by 47 m. Three faults were behind the difference:

1. **Earth orientation mixed two IAU conventions**: an equinox-based precession-nutation matrix
   with the CIO-based Earth rotation angle, which turned the Earth by 0.49° in the model. The 48 h
   fit fell from ~2.6 m RMS to ~0.15 m when fixed, and an implausible ~80 nm/s² radiation-pressure
   term vanished.
2. **Adaptive integration was noisy**: a 0.01 nm/s² change in radiation pressure "moved" the orbit
   by half a metre because the step sizes changed. Fixed 120 s steps make the result smooth.
3. **The fit stopped where it happened to be** (an evaluation cap, an ill-conditioned problem):
   now Levenberg-Marquardt on scaled unknowns, a priori constraints on the four secondary
   radiation-pressure terms, run to a 0.1 mm step. A fit from scratch and one started from a
   poor guess now agree to 0.2 mm after seven days (they were 350 m apart).

For comparison, reusing a real broadcast record past its validity reached a median range error of
~58 m after 12 hours and ~120 m after 22 (orbit off by ~390 m).

Notes:

- The force-model fit to the 48 h IGS arc is 0.12-0.26 m RMS for every satellite, with
  radiation-pressure terms of usual size (D0 about -100 nm/s², the others within a few).
- Range error grows faster than orbit error: the clock, a straight line through 48 h of IGS
  estimates, is now most of it.
- The first day uses the IGS's own prediction directly (fitted to 0.1 m), which is why it is so
  much better than days 1-5.
- Days 5-7 were not graded: BKG keeps only about a week of files.
- Not measured on a receiver.


## Later the same day: tides, clocks and Bulletin A

With the Rust program, each change was measured on its own against the same backtest, then the
final settings against a second, independent one (IGS orbit of 2026-10-14 00:00, graded over 4
days). Look-ahead was removed first: a run now sees only broadcasts up to 4 h after the IGS
prediction starts, as it would in practice (it changed nothing here).

**Where the error was.** At days 4-5, 98% of the squared range error was satellite clock: G06's
prediction was 21 m (70 ns) out while its orbit was within 1.8 m.

**Solid Earth tide** (IERS 2010 step 1, k2): orbit 3D at 3.5-4 days 2.79 to 2.64 m, neutral at day
5, range error unchanged. Kept, being physically right.

**Clock models**, range error at 4.5-5 days (median / 95% / worst), backtest 1:

| clock prediction | backtest 1, 4.5-5 d | backtest 2, 3.5-4 d |
| --- | --- | --- |
| line through 48 h of IGS clocks (before) | 4.73 / 17.2 / 25.2 m | 2.87 / 12.9 / 15.9 m |
| line plus 12/6/4/3 h harmonics | 4.75 / 17.2 / 25.2 m | |
| line through broadcast clocks, 1 / 2 / 4 / 6 days | 4.58-6.17 / 18-38 / 22-44 m | |
| quadratic through broadcast clocks, 3 days | 2.50 / 12.0 / 18.9 m | 2.35 / 11.3 / 14.0 m |
| quadratic, 4 days | 1.90 / 13.1 / 21.3 m | 1.64 / 6.8 / 10.1 m |
| **quadratic, 5 days (chosen)** | **2.15 / 12.3 / 14.7 m** | **1.18 / 6.8 / 7.9 m** |
| quadratic, 6 days | 2.43 / 13.1 / 22.6 m | 1.17 / 7.8 / 10.7 m |

GPS clocks age, so a line over more days does worse and a quadratic does much better. A robust
refit (dropping points more than four robust sigmas out) found nothing to drop.

**Earth rotation.** IERS Bulletin A (No. 041, 8 October) predicted UT1 to 0.12-0.28 ms over 4-5
days where extrapolating the IGS length of day was 0.6-0.8 ms out. But the orbits against the
broadcasts were no better: orbit 3D at 4.5-5 days 4.98 m extrapolated, 5.56 m with Bulletin A,
5.18 m with the UT1 and pole the IGS measured afterwards. Earth rotation is not what limits the
orbits; the force model is. Bulletin A is the default anyway, being the correct input.

## Radiation pressure and the remaining orbit error

At days 4-5 the orbit error is almost all along-track (median 4.7 m, radial 0.3 m, cross-track
0.7 m), growing roughly with the square of time, and mostly in the same direction for every
satellite (signed median -4.1 m). It does not depend on eclipse season (correlation with |beta|
-0.1). Orbit 3D at 4.5-5 days, backtest 1, for each thing tried:

| change | orbit 3D | range error median / 95% / worst |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECOM1 (before) | 5.56 m | 2.21 / 12.3 / 14.6 m |
| relativity (Schwarzschild) | 5.56 m | 2.21 / 12.3 / 14.6 m |
| GM 3.986004415e14 (EGM2008, IGS) instead of 3.986004418e14 | 5.56 m | 2.21 / 12.3 / 14.6 m |
| gravity to degree and order 6 | 5.43 m | 2.17 / 12.3 / 14.8 m |
| 50 s integration step instead of 100 s | 5.56 m | 2.21 / 12.3 / 14.6 m |
| fit the IGS's measured day only | 31.5 m | 6.56 / 13.3 / 17.4 m |
| ECOM1, priors 10x looser | 5.58 m | 2.22 / 12.4 / 14.6 m |
| ECOM1, priors 2-5x tighter | 5.89 m | 2.24 / 12.2 / 14.7 m |
| **ECOM2, extra terms at 5 nm/s²** | **4.97 m** | **2.19 / 12.3 / 14.6 m** |
| ECOM2, extra terms at 30 nm/s² | 5.07 m | 2.23 / 12.3 / 14.7 m |

ECOM2 also helped backtest 2 (orbit 3D at 4-4.5 days 3.46 to 3.07 m), so it is the default, with
relativity (no effect, but physically right). Range error barely moves because along-track error
counts about seven times less than radial or clock error.
